## 1. Experimental Architecture & Two-Path Source Topology

Rather than claiming three physically separate client machines, these experiments used **two distinct source IP paths** reaching `eth0` on `<ORIGIN_SERVER_IP>`:

1. **Path A  -  Direct-to-Origin Connection-Pressure Generator & External SSH Management Probes (`<CLIENT_LOAD_IP>` -> `<ORIGIN_SERVER_IP>:443`, `:22`, `:2222`):**
   - Connects directly to `<ORIGIN_SERVER_IP>:443` (`SNI: prometheusdynamics.ca`) from public IPv4 `<CLIENT_LOAD_IP>` (**un-exempted** in `/etc/argus/whitelist.conf` during Step 2).
   - Opens and holds `N` concurrent TCP sockets (150, 350, 800, 1,200 offered) during the 20 s active load window (`T = 5.0 s .. 25.0 s`) while 4-16 concurrent HTTPS workers continuously request the homepage directly from origin.
   - Simultaneously executes external authenticated SSH commands from the **same public IP (`<CLIENT_LOAD_IP>`)** on both port 22 and port 2222 at `T = 2.0 s` (`pre_load`), `T = 12.0 s` & `T = 18.0 s` (`active_flood`), and `T = 32.0 s` (`post_load_recovery`). Because the SSH probes share `<CLIENT_LOAD_IP>` with the load generator, they directly test whether management SSH from an actively banned source IP remains operational while `:443` traffic from that same IP is dropped by XDP and severed via `ss -K`.
2. **Path B  -  Legitimate Visitor HTTPS Probes via Cloudflare Dynamic Pass-Through (`<EDGE_PROXY_IP_A>` / `<EDGE_PROXY_IP_B>` -> `<ORIGIN_SERVER_IP>:443`):**
   - Requests `https://prometheusdynamics.ca/?visitor_probe=<trial_id>_<seq>_<ns>` (`Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache`, `Accept-Encoding: identity`) at a continuous open-loop rate of 2 RPS (80 probes over 40 s: 5 s pre-load, 20 s active load, 15 s post-load recovery).
   - Every response is verified for `HTTP 200`, `cf-cache-status: DYNAMIC` (confirming cache-bypassed origin pass-through to NGINX on `<ORIGIN_SERVER_IP>:443`), and full normalized homepage SHA-256 (90,656 bytes).
   - Arrives at `eth0` on `<ORIGIN_SERVER_IP>` from Cloudflare edge IPv4 addresses (`<EDGE_PROXY_IP_A>` / `<EDGE_PROXY_IP_B>`), which are **also un-exempted** in `/etc/argus/whitelist.conf` (all Cloudflare CIDRs in `whitelist.conf` are commented out).

## 2. Step 2A Results: Responsiveness Under Single-Source Connection Pressure (`Run 1` 4-Tier Sweep)

### 2.1 Scope of Findings & Explicitly Separated Failure and Recovery Criteria

1. **Responsiveness Under Pressure vs. Website Crash:**
   - In the initial 12-trial sweep (`Run 1`), **every configuration returned 40/40 (100%) valid `HTTP 200` visitor responses during active load and 30/30 (100%) during post-load recovery** (and across all 18 trials in the 3-run repeatability suite, 719/720 active-load probes returned `HTTP 200`).
   - Accordingly, these experiments **do not reproduce an unrecoverable website crash** requiring a manual restart. Instead, they measure **how effectively each configuration maintains visitor response times within the 500 ms target and prevents SSH management timeouts** under single-source connection pressure.
2. **Reconciliation of Saved Failure Flags (`explicit_failure_criteria`):**
   - The original machine-computed field `reached_failure_threshold` (`criterion_original_code_http200_outage_failed`) checked only for `< 80%` valid `HTTP 200` responses in a 5-second (10-probe) window or `>= 3` consecutive HTTP failures/timeouts, and forgot to OR-in SSH probe failures. It is preserved unmodified (`false` in all 12 `Run 1` trials).
   - Alongside it, `step2_pressure_comparison.json` and `step2_350_800_repeatability.json` report four explicitly named criteria:
     - `criterion_500ms_overall_active_flood_below_80pct`: `< 80.0%` (`< 32/40`) of active-load visitor probes completed within 500 ms.
     - `criterion_500ms_sliding_5s_window_below_80pct`: Any sliding 5-second (10-probe) window during active load with `< 8/10` (`< 80%`) probes completed within 500 ms.
     - `criterion_active_load_ssh_failed`: Any external SSH probe during active load (`T = 12 s` or `T = 18 s` on port 22 or 2222) failed or timed out (`> 6.0 s`).
     - `criterion_any_phase_ssh_failed`: Any external SSH probe across all phases (`pre_load`, `active_flood`, `post_load_recovery`) failed or timed out.
3. **Separation of Post-Load HTTP 200 Service from Timeline Recovery to the 500-ms Target (`recovery_timeline_analysis`):**
   - The original `recovery_time_from_flood_end_s: 0.0` field checked only whether all 30/30 post-load probes returned `HTTP 200`.
   - In the corrected analysis, **post-load `HTTP 200` availability (30/30)** and **post-load `<= 500 ms` count (`X/30`)** are reported separately from **sustained 15.0 s (30-consecutive-probe) `<= 500 ms` streak recovery** computed directly from the 80-probe timeline (`first_500ms_degradation_probe_s` to `first_15s_500ms_streak_start_probe_s`). Whenever a trial experiences a `> 500 ms` probe and does not subsequently achieve 30 consecutive probes (15.0 s) within 500 ms before the 40.0 s trial ends, `sustained_15s_500ms_streak_status` is explicitly marked **`"not observed within the measurement window"`** (`null`), alongside the longest consecutive `<= 500 ms` streak actually observed (`max_consecutive_within_500ms_duration_s`).

### 3.2 What the 3-Run Repeatability Data Establishes
1. **Consistent Socket Severance and Request Blocking:** In all 6/6 repeated `argus` trials across 350 and 800 connections (`plus` Tier 4), `argus` severed the flood generator's `:443` sockets down to 23-29 total server sockets and blocked **100% (0/860)** of the flood generator's HTTPS requests, whereas `reference` and `tuned` held 381-844 sockets open on NGINX and processed 100% of the flood requests.
2. **Lowest Median (`p50`) Visitor Latency in Every Run:** Across all 6 comparisons (3 runs at 350 connections + 3 runs at 800 connections), `argus` achieved the lowest `p50` visitor response time in every single run (280.6 - 310.7 ms, vs. 311.7 - 411.4 ms for `tuned` and 341.9 - 472.6 ms for `reference`), and was the only configuration with **0/6 runs falling below 80% overall `<= 500 ms` compliance** (plus 0 runs below 80% at Tier 4 1,200 offered connections).
3. **Where Tuning vs. Argus Differ Most:**
   - At **350 connections** (below `reference`'s 768 `worker_connections` ceiling), `tuned` alone provides no advantage over `reference` (77.50% vs. 85.00% pooled `<= 500 ms`) because both configurations serve all 350 concurrent connections and ~700-900 TLS/HTTP flood requests on the single 1 vCPU core. Here `argus` provides the largest improvement (**90.83% pooled, 95.0% median**).
   - At **800 connections** (above `reference`'s 768 `worker_connections` limit), raising `worker_connections` to 16,384 in `tuned` prevents the socket-table exhaustion seen in `reference` (71.67% pooled `<= 500 ms`), lifting `tuned` to 91.67% pooled (90.0% median) and `argus` to 92.50% pooled (90.0% median, with lower `p50` latency and `96%+` fewer active `:443` sockets).
   - At **1,200 offered connections** (911-914 established before the generator ceiling, with 16 active HTTPS flood workers), CPU contention from the higher worker count degraded `tuned` to 65.0% (26/40) and `reference` to 52.5% (21/40), whereas `argus` severed the flood sockets and maintained **92.5% (37/40)** within 500 ms.

### 6.3 Summary of True Value vs. True Limitation
1. **True Value (Single-Source Connection Pressure `> 200` Conns):**
   - Over-provisioning from 4 USD/mo (1 vCPU, 512 MB) to 18 USD/mo (2 vCPU, 2 GB) or 48 USD/mo (4 vCPU, 8 GB) **without Argus** did not solve single-source connection floods: across 3 runs, the 18 USD/mo and 48 USD/mo servers without Argus achieved **76.7%-82.5% within 500 ms at 350 connections** and **65.0%-82.5% at 800 connections** (and dropped to **22.5%-32.5%** on the 48 USD/mo server under 800 connections + 32 workers).
   - Meanwhile, the **4 USD/mo (1 vCPU, 512 MB) droplet with Argus** achieved **90.8% (350 conns)**, **92.5% (800 conns)**, and **92.5% (1,200 attempted conns)** - saving **14 USD/month (`168 USD/year`, 78%) vs. 18 USD/mo** and **44 USD/month (`528 USD/year`, 91.7%) vs. 48 USD/mo**.
   - On the 48 USD/mo (4 vCPU, 8 GB) server itself, enabling Argus lifted visitor responses within 500 ms under the heavy 800-connection / 32-worker flood from **22.5% (`tuned`) / 32.5% (`reference`) to 97.5% (39/40)**.
2. **True Limitation (Sub-Threshold High-Rate Traffic & Legitimate Compute Growth):**
   - When a client holds `< 200` connections (150 client sockets / 197-199 total `:443` sockets) and drives heavy request throughput (24 workers, ~1,280 responses = `~46 Mbps`), Argus's 200-connection threshold does not fire (0 bans), and visitor latency degrades (`37.5%-57.5% <= 500 ms`).
   - Likewise, Argus cannot replace CPU cores or RAM required for legitimate compute/database growth or distributed below-threshold floods; it must be paired with L7 request-rate limits (`limit_req`) and upstream edge protection.
